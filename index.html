About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Political Science at the Ohio State University (OSU), where I study comparative politics and methodology. My research interests are in infrastructure, water governance and politics, accountability, climate change, and development

My dissertation, "[Structural Vulnerability in Low-Visibility Infrastructure: Understanding Institutional Design's influence on Sewerage Governance]," investigates the governance of water and sewage infrastructure, specifically why some systems deliver clean, reliable service while others fail, and what regulation and accountability have to do with it. I'm broadly interested in studying when and why capable states fail. I use both quantitative and qualitative methods. Before starting my Ph.D., I earned a MA in Political Science at the University of Chicago and a Master of International Studies at Seoul National Univeristy (SNU).

Outside of academia, I enjoy crocheting and baking (although none of my creations could or should be sold).

Research

Dissertation project[Structural Vulnerability in Low-Visibility Infrastructure: Understanding Institutional Design's influence on Sewerage Governance]
Dissertation project · in progress
A comparative study of why water and sewage governance succeeds in some systems and fails in others, using England's privatized model and Scotland's public model as paired cases. The project argues that regulatory environment and accountability structures, rather than ownership form,determine system performance, combining longitudinal data analysis with comparative institutional case study.
